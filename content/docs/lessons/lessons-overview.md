# Lessons Module

The Lessons module takes teachers from planning all the way through live delivery and student study. Create rich lesson content, teach it in class, publish it for students to review at home, and track who's keeping up — all in one place.

## Creating a Lesson

1. Navigate to **Lessons** from the sidebar and click **"New Lesson"**.
2. Fill in the basics: **title, subject, class/grade, topic, and learning objectives**.
3. Build the lesson body using the rich-text editor — add text, images, embedded videos, file attachments, and formatted equations.
4. Save your lesson as a **Draft** to continue editing later, or move it through the publication workflow.

> **Tip:** Link your lesson to a specific week in your Scheme of Work so coverage tracking updates automatically.

## Lesson Workflow: Draft to Published

Every lesson follows a clear lifecycle:

- **Draft** — Work in progress, visible only to you.
- **Ready for Review** — Submit the lesson to a department head or academic coordinator for feedback.
- **Approved** — The reviewer signs off and the lesson is ready to teach.
- **Published** — The lesson content is visible to students and parents.

You can move lessons forward or back through these stages from the lesson detail page.

## Teaching Mode

When it's time to deliver the lesson, open **Teaching Mode**:

- Your lesson content displays in a clean, distraction-free view optimised for projection or screen-sharing.
- Navigate through content sections with forward/back controls.
- Mark the lesson as **"Taught"** when you finish, recording the date and updating coverage stats.

Teaching Mode keeps you focused on delivery while EduSentrix handles the record-keeping in the background.

## Lesson Bank

The Lesson Bank is a shared library of approved lessons that any teacher in the school can reuse.

- Toggle **"Save to Lesson Bank"** when creating or approving a lesson.
- Browse the bank by **subject, grade, topic, or keyword**.
- Import a bank lesson into your own class — it creates a copy you can customise without affecting the original.

> **Tip:** The Lesson Bank is perfect for onboarding new teachers. They get a head start with proven content from experienced colleagues.

## Collaboration & Comments

Co-planning is built in. On any lesson, colleagues can:

- Leave **comments** on specific sections of the lesson content.
- **Tag** another teacher with `@name` to draw their attention.
- View the full **comment thread** alongside the lesson, making feedback easy to follow and act on.

Comments are great for department-wide lesson review sessions or paired teaching partnerships.

## Flashcard Decks

Turn key concepts into study aids right inside the lesson:

1. Open a lesson and click **"Flashcards"**.
2. Add cards with a **term/question** on the front and a **definition/answer** on the back.
3. Organise cards into decks by topic.

When the lesson is published, students can study the flashcards in a spaced-repetition mode from their Student Portal, reinforcing retention outside of class.

## Publishing for Students

When you publish a lesson, it appears in the **Student Portal** under the relevant subject. Students can:

- Read the full lesson content at their own pace
- View embedded media and attachments
- Study linked flashcard decks
- Mark the lesson as **"Completed"** to track their own progress

### Progress Tracking

EduSentrix records which students have viewed and completed each lesson. Teachers see:

- **Completion percentage** per lesson
- A **student-by-student breakdown** showing who has viewed, started, or completed the lesson
- **Time spent** — approximate reading/study duration

This data helps you identify students who may need extra support before an assessment.

## How Parents View Lessons

Parents with EduSentrix accounts can see their ward's published lessons from the **Parent Portal**:

- Browse lessons by subject and date
- Read the lesson content alongside their child
- Monitor their ward's completion status

> **Tip:** Encourage parents to review lessons with their wards at home — it strengthens the school-home learning connection.

## Lesson Analytics

The analytics dashboard gives teachers and administrators deeper insight:

- **Lessons created vs. taught** — Are plans translating into delivery?
- **Coverage by subject and class** — Which areas are on track?
- **Student engagement** — Completion rates and time-on-task trends
- **Bank usage** — How often are shared lessons being reused?

Filter analytics by **term, subject, class, or teacher** to drill into the data that matters.

## Best Practices

1. **Write clear objectives** — Students and reviewers should know exactly what the lesson aims to achieve.
2. **Use multimedia** — A short video or diagram can explain a concept far more effectively than text alone.
3. **Create flashcards** — Even a small deck of 5–10 cards per lesson dramatically improves recall.
4. **Publish promptly** — Students benefit most when lesson content is available for review the same day it's taught.
5. **Check progress weekly** — Glance at completion data to spot disengaged students early.
6. **Contribute to the bank** — Sharing your best lessons benefits the whole school.
