# Curricula Management

Curricula are the backbone of academic planning in EduSentrix. By defining and managing your curriculum framework within the platform, you ensure that lesson notes, schemes of work, and assessments are all aligned to recognised standards.

## What Are Curricula in EduSentrix?

In EduSentrix, a curriculum is a structured framework that defines what students should learn at each level. It serves as the reference point for all academic content on the platform.

- Curricula represent recognised frameworks such as **NaCCA** (Ghana's National Council for Curriculum and Assessment), **Cambridge**, **IB** (International Baccalaureate), or any custom framework your school follows.
- Each curriculum is broken down into a hierarchy of **nodes** that describe learning expectations in detail.
- Your school connects to one or more curricula, and everything — from lesson notes to exam questions — can be mapped back to these standards.

> **Tip:** Most schools in Ghana will use the NaCCA curriculum. International schools may use Cambridge or IB alongside or instead of NaCCA.

## Managing Curriculum Nodes

Curriculum nodes are the building blocks of a curriculum framework. They form a tree structure from broad categories down to specific learning outcomes.

- **Strands** — the broadest categories within a subject (e.g. "Number and Algebra" in Mathematics).
- **Sub-strands** — more specific groupings within a strand (e.g. "Fractions" under Number and Algebra).
- **Content standards** — describe what a student should know or be able to do (e.g. "Demonstrate understanding of equivalent fractions").
- **Indicators** — the most specific level, describing measurable actions or outcomes (e.g. "Use diagrams to show that 1/2 = 2/4").

### Working with Nodes

- Navigate to **Curricula → [Your Curriculum]** to view the full node tree.
- Expand any strand to see its sub-strands, content standards, and indicators.
- Admins can **add, edit, or remove** nodes to keep the curriculum up to date.
- Search and filter to quickly find specific standards or indicators.

> **Tip:** If GES releases a curriculum update, you can edit your nodes directly in EduSentrix rather than starting from scratch.

## Mapping Curriculum Subjects

Connect your school's subjects to the curriculum framework so that content and assessments align automatically.

- Go to **Curricula → Subject Mapping**.
- Link each of your school's subjects (e.g. "Maths" or "Integrated Science") to the corresponding curriculum subject.
- Once mapped, teachers creating lesson notes or exams will see the relevant standards and indicators for their subject and level.
- This mapping ensures consistency — every teacher working on the same subject references the same standards.

## How Curricula Connect to Schemes of Work

Schemes of work are term-level plans that break the curriculum into weekly teaching segments.

- When a teacher creates a scheme of work, they select the curriculum standards they plan to cover each week.
- This creates a direct link between the weekly plan and the national (or school) standards.
- Admins and heads of department can review schemes of work to verify full curriculum coverage across the term.

## How Curricula Connect to Lesson Notes

Lesson notes are the day-to-day teaching plans, and they tie directly to the curriculum.

- Each lesson note can be tagged with the specific **content standard** and **indicator** it addresses.
- This makes it easy to see whether a lesson is aligned with the expected learning outcomes.
- Leo AI uses curriculum data to generate lesson notes that are already aligned to the right standards.

> **Tip:** Tag every lesson note with a curriculum indicator. Over time, this builds a complete map of what's been taught and where gaps might exist.

## Best Practices

- Set up your curriculum at the beginning of the academic year, before teachers start planning.
- Encourage teachers to always link their lessons and exams to curriculum nodes — this enables powerful reporting on curriculum coverage.
- Review the curriculum tree periodically to ensure it reflects the latest national or international standards.
- Use curriculum coverage reports to identify topics that may have been under-taught during the term.
