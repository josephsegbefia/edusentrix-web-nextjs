# Examinations & Question Bank

Create, review, and publish exam papers — all from one place. The Examinations module in EduSentrix helps teachers build professional assessment papers while the Question Bank ensures your best questions are always reusable.

## Creating an Exam Paper

1. Go to **Examinations** in the sidebar and click **"New Exam"**.
2. Fill in the basics: **title, subject, class/grade, term, and date**.
3. Set the **total marks** and **duration** for the paper.
4. Click **Create** to open the paper editor.

> **Tip:** You can attach instructions for invigilators (e.g., "Allow use of calculators") in the exam settings panel.

## Organising with Sections

Most papers are divided into sections — for example, *Section A: Objective* and *Section B: Essay*. Sections help you:

- Group questions by type or difficulty
- Set per-section instructions (e.g., "Answer ALL questions in this section")
- Assign marks independently to each section

To add a section, click **"Add Section"** inside the paper editor and give it a title and optional instructions.

## Adding Questions

Within each section, click **"Add Question"** to choose a question type:

- **Multiple Choice** — Options A–D (or more) with a single correct answer
- **Multi-Select** — Multiple correct answers from a list
- **True / False** — Simple binary choice
- **Short Answer** — A brief text response
- **Structured** — Multi-part questions with sub-questions and individual marks
- **Essay** — Open-ended, long-form response
- **Fill-in-the-Blank** — Sentence completion

For each question you can set:

- **Mark allocation** — How many marks the question carries
- **Correct answer / marking guide** — Used for auto-marking or as a reference for markers
- **Bloom's taxonomy level** — Optionally tag cognitive level (Knowledge, Application, Analysis, etc.)

### Reordering Questions

Drag and drop questions within a section to change their order. You can also move a question from one section to another by dragging it across section boundaries.

## The Question Bank

The Question Bank is a school-wide library of vetted questions that any teacher can draw from.

### Adding Questions to the Bank

- When creating exam questions, toggle **"Save to Question Bank"** to store a copy.
- You can also add questions directly from **Question Bank → New Question**.

### Using Bank Questions in an Exam

1. Inside a section, click **"Import from Bank"**.
2. Filter by **subject, topic, question type, or difficulty**.
3. Select one or more questions and click **Add to Paper**.
4. Adjust marks or wording if needed — edits apply only to your paper, not the bank copy.

> **Tip:** The Question Bank is especially powerful for standardised mid-term and end-of-term exams where departments want consistency across classes.

## Review & Approval Workflow

Exam papers follow an approval process similar to schemes of work:

1. **Draft** — The teacher builds the paper.
2. **Submitted** — The paper is sent for departmental or admin review.
3. **Reviewed** — A reviewer checks content accuracy, mark allocation, and curriculum alignment.
4. **Approved / Returned** — The reviewer approves the paper or returns it with comments.

Reviewers can leave inline feedback on individual questions, making it easy to pinpoint issues.

## Exporting & Printing

Once an exam is approved, you can generate a print-ready **PDF**:

- Click **"Export PDF"** on the approved paper.
- Choose formatting options: font size, margins, school logo placement, and whether to include an answer sheet.
- The PDF is generated instantly and can be downloaded or sent directly to your school's printer queue.

> **Tip:** Use the "Answer Key" export option to generate a separate marker's guide with correct answers and marking rubrics.

## Mark Allocation Summary

The paper editor displays a live **mark summary** showing:

- Marks per section
- Total marks vs. your target
- A warning if the totals don't match

This helps you catch allocation errors before the paper goes to review.

## Best Practices

1. **Use the bank** — Building from existing vetted questions saves time and ensures quality.
2. **Balance question types** — Mix objective and subjective items to assess different cognitive levels.
3. **Review as a team** — Have at least one colleague review your paper before approval.
4. **Tag questions** — Adding topic and difficulty tags to bank questions makes future searching much faster.
5. **Preview the PDF** — Always check the exported PDF for formatting issues before printing.
